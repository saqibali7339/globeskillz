Shopify Plus vs BigCommerce for Mid-Market DTC Brands

Shopify Plus and BigCommerce Performance are the two enterprise options most mid-market DTC brands choose between. One has the biggest app ecosystem and a published price; the other has a lower starting license, no platform fee on your gateway, and more control over your storefront. This guide compares price, checkout, B2B, headless, apps, and SEO so you can pick the side that fits how your brand sells.

If your direct-to-consumer brand does roughly $1 million to $10 million a year online, you have outgrown the standard plans. The next step is usually one of two platforms: Shopify Plus or BigCommerce Performance, the enterprise tier BigCommerce renamed from Enterprise in June 2026. Both are hosted, both handle high volume, and both cost real money. The difference is what you get for the money, and which trade-offs you are willing to live with. This guide compares them on price, checkout, B2B, headless, apps, and SEO, so you can pick the one that fits how your brand actually sells.

The short answer

Pick Shopify Plus if you want the biggest app ecosystem, the simplest checkout customization, and a published price you can budget against. Pick BigCommerce Performance if you want a lower starting license, no extra platform fee on your payment gateway, and more freedom over your storefront code and URL structure.

If your revenue is under about $1 million a year, you probably need neither. BigCommerce Scale costs $299 a month billed annually, and Shopify Advanced costs $399 a month, and either one covers most brands at that size. The rest of this guide explains where the enterprise tiers earn their keep, and where they do not.

What "mid-market DTC" means for this comparison

For this guide, a mid-market DTC brand means roughly $1 million to $10 million in annual online sales, a small team, and growth limited by operations rather than demand. You are past the stage where a theme and a few apps carry the whole store, but you do not have an in-house engineering team either.

Three things matter most at this size. First, checkout conversion, because a small lift there is worth more than any new feature. Second, the cost of everything around the platform: apps, payment processing, and developer time. Third, speed: how fast you can launch a change without waiting on a developer. Keep those three in mind while you read the comparison, because the cheaper license is not always the cheaper platform.

Pricing: what each platform actually costs

Shopify Plus publishes its price. It costs $2,300 a month on a three-year term or $2,500 a month on a one-year term for standard setups. At very high volume, roughly above $800,000 in monthly sales, Shopify moves the largest stores to a variable platform fee based on revenue, and the exact rate is set in the contract rather than published. This Shopify Plus pricing breakdown lists the term rates from Shopify's own Help Center.

There is one more Shopify Plus charge to know about. If you use a third-party payment gateway instead of Shopify Payments, Plus adds a 0.2 percent fee on those transactions. With Shopify Payments there is no extra platform fee, only the card processing rate.

BigCommerce Performance does not publish a fixed price. It starts at $1,499 a month billed annually, and the final price is a custom quote based on your volume and requirements. One important difference: BigCommerce charges no extra platform fee on top of your payment gateway's own rate, whichever gateway you use.

On the published starting prices alone, Performance looks cheaper: $17,988 a year at its floor against $27,600 a year for Plus on a three-year term. But the license is only part of the bill. Payment processing, apps, and developer time usually cost more than the license on either platform, so compare the total, not the headline number. And check the tier below first: a brand doing $2 million a year can still be cheaper on BigCommerce Scale than on Performance, because Scale's $299 a month plus 0.9 percent on sales above $400,000 a year can undercut the Performance floor. This BigCommerce vs Shopify Plus comparison works through the same arithmetic with prices checked in November 2026.

Checkout and customization

Checkout is where Shopify Plus is strongest. You get full checkout customization through checkout UI extensions and Shopify Functions, plus Shopify Flow for automation, unlimited staff accounts, and nine expansion stores under the same contract for extra storefronts or international markets. Shopify Scripts, the old way to customize checkout, stopped running on June 30, 2026, so any guide that still recommends Scripts is out of date.

BigCommerce checkout is customizable too, but it usually takes more developer work to get the same result. Where BigCommerce wins on customization is the storefront itself: its open SaaS approach gives you API access to the catalog, cart, and checkout, so a developer can build almost any front end on top of it. Shopify's theme system, built on Liquid, is easier to work with and has a far bigger theme market, but it is also more opinionated about how a store is built.

For a DTC brand that sells mostly to consumers and wants checkout apps, discount logic, and post-purchase offers without custom code, Plus is the easier platform. For a brand that wants a fully custom storefront and is willing to pay a developer for it, BigCommerce gives more room.

B2B, multi-storefront, and headless

Both platforms handle B2B, multi-storefront, and headless builds, but they lean in different directions. Shopify Plus includes native B2B catalogs with company-specific pricing, and its nine expansion stores cover separate storefronts for markets or brands. BigCommerce is the stronger multi-storefront platform: it was built for running several storefronts from one catalog, and its headless tooling is more mature.

On B2B, check what is actually included before you sign. BigCommerce's B2B Edition is an add-on on some contracts, so a quote that looks cheap may not include the B2B features you assumed. Shopify Plus includes its B2B tools in the platform fee. If wholesale or trade orders are a large share of your revenue, get both vendors to confirm the B2B scope in writing.

For headless, BigCommerce is the more open choice. Its APIs cover the whole commerce flow, and it has been the default headless pick for brands that want a custom front end for years. Shopify has Hydrogen and Oxygen for headless builds, but Plus is still an opinionated platform first, and a headless build on it means working around more of Shopify's defaults.

Apps and ecosystem

Shopify has the largest app store and the largest agency and developer pool in ecommerce by a wide margin. Most DTC tools launch on Shopify first, and some never build a BigCommerce version at all. That matters when you need a specific reviews app, loyalty tool, or subscription integration: on Plus you will almost always find it, and on BigCommerce you may have to build it or live without it.

BigCommerce has more than 1,200 apps, which covers the mainstream tools most stores need. The gap shows up in the long tail: niche integrations, newer AI tools, and category-specific apps. Hiring is the other gap. Shopify developers are easy to find at every price point, while BigCommerce developers are scarcer, which means slower hiring and higher rates when you need custom work.

Ecosystem size is the single biggest practical difference between the two platforms. It is also the hardest to fix after you choose, because your apps, your integrations, and your team's knowledge all live in one ecosystem. This side-by-side comparison puts the same point in a feature table if you want to check it against your own requirements.

SEO: where each platform helps or hurts

Both platforms handle the SEO basics well: SSL, automatic sitemaps, editable titles and meta descriptions, and redirects. Neither one will rank your store by itself, and neither one will hold you back if the fundamentals are right.

The real difference is control. Shopify locks in its URL structure: products live under /products/ and collections under /collections/, and you cannot change those paths. BigCommerce lets you set custom URL structures, which helps when you are migrating from another platform and want to keep your existing URLs instead of redirecting all of them. That control is worth real money on a migration, because every redirect is a small ranking risk.

On the other side, Shopify has the bigger SEO app ecosystem, with more tools for schema, internal linking, and bulk editing. If your SEO work is mostly content and on-page optimization, either platform does the job. If you are migrating a large catalog with established URLs, BigCommerce's URL control is a genuine advantage. Migration: what moving costs

Moving between Plus and Performance, or onto either one from a standard plan, is a development and SEO job, not a weekend task. The catalog, customers, and order history move over, but the theme has to be rebuilt, apps have to be replaced or reconnected, and every URL needs a redirect map so you do not lose rankings.

The redirect map is the part that decides whether the migration succeeds. A store with a few hundred products can map URLs by hand. A store with thousands of products and years of backlinks needs a proper audit first, so the URLs that actually earn traffic are the ones that get redirected correctly. Which one should you pick?

Pick Shopify Plus if you sell mostly direct to consumers, you want the biggest choice of apps, you want checkout customization and Flow automation without custom code, and you want a published price you can put in a budget. That describes most mid-market DTC brands, which is why Plus is the default pick in this segment.

Pick BigCommerce Performance if you need serious headless or multi-storefront depth, you use a specific payment gateway and want no platform fee on it, B2B is a large share of your revenue, or you want control over your URL structure and storefront code. That describes brands with complex catalogs, multiple brands or regions, or a large wholesale arm.

Pick neither yet if your revenue is under about $1 million a year. Shopify Advanced or BigCommerce Scale gives you almost everything you need at that size for a fraction of the price, and the money you save buys the apps and content that actually grow revenue. Upgrade when checkout limits, B2B needs, or app gaps force the move, not before.

Frequently asked questions

Is BigCommerce Enterprise still called Enterprise?

No. BigCommerce renamed its Enterprise plan to Performance on June 1, 2026, as part of a wider plan restructure. You will still see the Enterprise name in older guides and in some of BigCommerce's own pages, but Performance is the current name for the same tier.

Is Shopify Plus worth it for a $1 million brand?

Usually not yet. At $1 million a year, Shopify Advanced or BigCommerce Scale covers most brands for a fraction of the price. Plus earns its fee when you need checkout customization, B2B catalogs, expansion stores, or Flow automation at a scale the standard plans cannot handle.

Does BigCommerce charge transaction fees?

BigCommerce charges no extra platform fee on top of your payment gateway's own rate, on any plan. You still pay the gateway's processing rate. Shopify Plus adds a 0.2 percent fee when you use a third-party gateway instead of Shopify Payments.

Can I migrate from Shopify Plus to BigCommerce, or back?

Yes. The catalog, customers, and order history can be moved in either direction. The theme has to be rebuilt on the new platform, apps have to be replaced, and every URL needs a redirect. The redirect map is the part that protects your rankings, so it should be planned before the move, not after.

Which platform is better for SEO?

Both can rank well. BigCommerce gives you more control over URL structure, which helps on migrations. Shopify has the bigger SEO app ecosystem. The platform is rarely the reason a store does not rank: thin content, slow pages, and weak internal linking are the usual causes on either one.
