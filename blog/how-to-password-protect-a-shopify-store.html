How to Password Protect a Shopify Store

Keep your Shopify store hidden while you build it. Here is how to turn on the storefront password, write a good welcome message, and remove it at launch.

If your Shopify store is still being built, the last thing you want is real customers landing on half-finished product pages and a checkout that does not work yet. A storefront password solves this cleanly. Visitors see a simple page asking for a password, and only the people you give it to can browse the store.

Turning it on takes about two minutes and costs nothing. Here is exactly how to do it, plus how to make the password page look professional and when to switch it off.

Why store owners use a storefront password

Most owners turn on the storefront password while the store is under construction. Products are half written, photos are placeholders, and the payment setup is not finished. A password keeps the public out while you work.

It is also handy for private or wholesale stores. Some businesses only sell to approved retailers or a closed group of customers, and a shared password is the simplest gate. The same trick works when you are redesigning a live store and want a few hours of privacy.

Note that a storefront password locks the shopping pages only. It does not change anything about your admin login, which already has its own password.

Turn on the storefront password (step by step)

Log in to your Shopify admin. Go to Settings, then Preferences. Scroll down to the Password protect section. If you are on an older layout, you may find it under Online Store, then Preferences instead.

Tick the box to restrict access to visitors with the password. Type the password you want to use. Shopify also lets you add a short message for your visitors, which is the text shown on the password page. We will cover what to write in the next section.

Click Save. From that moment, anyone visiting your store sees the password page instead of your shop. You can test it yourself by opening your store in a private or incognito browser window.

Write a good password page message

Do not leave the default text untouched if real people might arrive. A short, honest message builds confidence instead of confusion. Say the store is being built, tell visitors roughly when it opens, and leave a way to reach you.

A simple example: Thanks for stopping by. We are putting the finishing touches on our new store and will be open soon. Questions? Email us at hello@yourstore.com. Keep it to two or three lines.

Avoid long paragraphs. People on a password page are usually in a hurry, and a wall of text makes the page look abandoned rather than under construction.

Customize the password page to match your brand

The password page uses your theme, so you can style it like the rest of the store. In your admin, go to Online Store, then Themes, and click Customize on your active theme.

At the top of the theme editor, switch the template dropdown to the password page. You can change the logo, headline, message, colors, and background image, just like any other page.

Small details matter here. A clean background and your logo on the password page tell visitors that a real business is behind it. That trust pays off when they come back on launch day.

Share access with the right people

You will want a few people to browse the full store before launch: your designer, a photographer, or a partner checking product descriptions. Send them the password directly by email or message.

Keep the password simple during the build phase so sharing is easy. Avoid reusing an important personal password. You can change it any time from the same Preferences page.

Remember that the storefront password is the same for everyone. Shopify does not offer individual passwords for visitors, so only share it with people you trust.

Remove the password when you are ready to launch

Launch day is simple. Go back to Settings, then Preferences, and untick the password protection box. Click Save, and the store opens to everyone immediately.

Before you remove it, run a quick final check. Open the store in an incognito window after switching the password off and click through a few pages, the cart, and the first checkout step. It is much easier to fix a broken link now than after customers find it.

If your store is on a trial plan, note that the password may be required by Shopify until you pick a paid plan. Once your subscription is active, you are free to remove it.

Frequently asked questions

Does a storefront password hurt my SEO?

Search engines cannot see the pages behind the password, so those pages will not rank while it is on. That is fine during a build, since half-finished pages should not rank anyway. Once you remove the password at launch, Google can crawl the site normally. The password page itself can be submitted in Search Console after launch.

What is the difference between the storefront password and my admin password?

The storefront password controls what shoppers see on your website. Your admin password is your login for the Shopify dashboard, where you manage products and orders. Changing one does not affect the other, and they should not be the same.

Can I give different passwords to different visitors?

No. Shopify uses one shared storefront password for all visitors. If you need individual accounts, look into Shopify apps for membership or wholesale pricing, which create per-customer logins.

Can customers still check out while the store is password protected?

No. While the storefront password is on, visitors cannot browse products or reach the checkout. Only after you share the password with them can they shop. When you are ready to take real orders, turn the password off.
