How Much Do Shopify Experts Cost?

There is no single Shopify expert rate. Freelancers charge $25 to $150 an hour, agencies $75 to $300, and the job itself matters more than the title. Here is how to read a quote and hire well.

Ask three Shopify experts what they charge and you will get three different answers. One quotes $25 an hour. Another quotes $150. A third will not give you a number at all until they see the job. All three can be honest, because Shopify expert covers everyone from a freelancer doing theme tweaks to an agency rebuilding a Plus store.

This guide gives you the real ranges for 2026: what freelancers and agencies charge per hour, what common jobs cost as fixed projects, and what moves a quote up or down. It also shows what Globe Skillz charges, so you can compare our rates against the market in the same place.

The short answer

A freelance Shopify expert usually charges $25 to $150 an hour, depending on experience and location. An agency usually charges $75 to $300 an hour. For a fixed project, a small theme job starts around $475, a full store built by a freelancer runs $500 to $10,000, and an agency build runs $5,000 to $40,000 or more.

Shopify itself sets no rate. The old Experts Marketplace closed in December 2023, and hiring now runs through the Shopify Partner Directory, where each partner sets its own price. That is why quotes vary so much: you are comparing different people, not different prices for the same thing.

Shopify expert hourly rates

Hourly rates track experience more than anything else. Published 2025 to 2026 marketplace guides put junior freelancers at $25 to $50 an hour, mid-level freelancers at $50 to $75, and senior specialists at $80 to $150 or more. The work matches the rate: juniors handle theme tweaks and simple setup, mid-level developers handle custom themes and app integrations, and senior specialists handle Plus builds, headless work, and performance problems.

Agencies charge more because you pay for a team, not one person. Boutique agencies run about $90 to $125 an hour, mid-sized agencies $125 to $175, and premium or Plus-focused agencies $200 an hour or more. On Upwork, the median Shopify developer bills about $20 an hour, with most between $15 and $29, rising to $95 an hour for the Expert tier. That median is useful context, but it is not a quote: the cheapest available developer is rarely the right one for a store that has to sell.

Location moves the rate as much as experience. North American freelancers typically charge $75 to $150 an hour and agencies $125 to $300. Eastern European developers run $30 to $70, and South Asian developers $15 to $40. A lower rate is not a worse developer, but the gap usually reflects local costs, not a bargain on the same work.

What experts charge by project type

Hourly rates tell you what a person costs. Fixed project prices tell you what a job costs, and most experts prefer to quote fixed prices once the scope is clear.

Small theme work, such as fixing a layout, adding a section, or cleaning up a product page, often starts around $475 on curated marketplaces. A full store build by a freelancer runs $500 to $10,000, depending on whether it is a theme setup with your content or a custom design with many products. The same build at an agency runs $5,000 to $40,000, and a Shopify Plus or headless build starts around $60,000. Migrations, speed work, and custom app builds are quoted per job, because two migrations are never the same size.

Monthly retainers are the third way experts charge. A freelancer who looks after your store month to month often charges a few hundred dollars a month. Agency retainers run $2,000 to $15,000 a month and usually cover design, development, and marketing together. If you only need one of those, a smaller retainer with a specialist costs less and does the job better.

What moves the price up or down

Five things move a quote more than the expert title does.

First, custom work versus theme setup. Setting up a bought theme with your products and content is fast. Custom design, custom Liquid code, and a checkout that behaves differently from the default are slow, and the price follows the hours.

Second, the number of products and pages. Ten products with clean data is a day of work. A thousand products with messy variants, images, and descriptions is a project, and migrations from another platform add data cleanup on top.

Third, integrations. Connecting a warehouse system, an accounting tool, or a marketing stack takes testing time, and every extra system is another place a sync can break.

Fourth, timeline. A two-week deadline costs more than a six-week one, because the expert has to turn other work away. Rush fees are normal, and a quote that ignores your deadline is a warning sign, not a bargain.

Fifth, who does the work after launch. A quote that includes testing, training, and a month of fixes is worth more than a cheaper quote that ends the day the site goes live.

Freelancer or agency: which one fits your job

Choose by the size of the job, not by the price tag. A freelancer is the right call for a defined job with a clear end: fix the theme, speed up the store, migrate the products, build the store from a theme. You get one experienced person, direct communication, and a lower rate. The risk is continuity: if they are busy or away, the work waits.

An agency is the right call when the job needs several skills at once: design, development, SEO, and marketing for a full rebuild, or a Plus store that has to handle real volume. You get a team, a project manager, and cover when someone is away. The cost is a higher rate and a slower start, because more people have to be briefed.

For ongoing work, the same split applies. A freelancer on retainer suits a store that needs regular small changes. An agency retainer suits a store that needs steady growth work across several areas. If you are not sure which you need, ask for a quote from both and compare what is actually included, not just the total.

How to hire a Shopify expert without overpaying

Start with a written scope before you ask for prices. List the pages, the products, the apps, and what done looks like. Experts quote against scope, and a vague brief produces vague quotes that grow later.

Check live stores they built, not screenshots. Open the store, click through it, and check it on your phone. Ask which parts they built themselves and which parts came from the theme.

Check the Shopify Partner Directory listing and any reviews on it. A partner with a real listing and real client stores is a safer hire than a profile with only promises.

Get the price in writing with milestones. A common split is 50 percent upfront and 50 percent on delivery for projects, and monthly in advance for retainers. Avoid anyone who wants the full amount before work starts, and avoid anyone who guarantees first-page rankings: nobody controls Google, and that promise is a warning sign.

Finally, compare the quote against the job, not against other quotes. The cheapest quote for a custom build is usually the one that left the hard parts out.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a Shopify expert charge per hour?

A freelance Shopify expert usually charges $25 to $150 an hour, depending on experience and location. Agencies usually charge $75 to $300 an hour. Junior freelancers start around $25 an hour, and senior specialists and premium agencies charge $150 to $200 an hour or more.

How much does it cost to hire a Shopify expert for a full store?

A full store built by a freelancer usually costs $500 to $10,000, depending on custom design and product count. The same build at an agency usually costs $5,000 to $40,000. A Shopify Plus or headless build starts around $60,000.

Where do I find a Shopify expert?

The Shopify Partner Directory is the main place to look. Shopify closed the old Experts Marketplace in December 2023, and partners now list their services and prices in the directory. Check live stores they built and read their reviews before you hire.

Is it worth hiring a Shopify expert?

Yes, when the job is bigger than a theme setting or an app can handle. An expert pays for itself on custom design, migrations, speed problems, and anything that touches checkout or revenue. For a small change, the theme editor or a low-cost app is often enough.
