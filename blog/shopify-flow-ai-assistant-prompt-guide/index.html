Shopify Flow AI Assistant Prompt Guide: What to Type to Build Any Workflow

Shopify Flow's AI assistant, Sidekick, drafts whole workflows from a plain-English sentence. This guide shows the prompt formula that works, copy-ready examples for the most common automations, and the checks to run before you turn anything on.

Shopify Flow turns repetitive store work into rules that run on their own: tag the order, email the team, hide the product. The hard part was never the idea. It was knowing the exact names of triggers, conditions, and actions, and stitching them together in the right order.

That is what the Flow AI assistant changes. You describe the automation in plain English and Sidekick, Shopify's built-in AI assistant, drafts the whole workflow for you. This guide shows the prompt formula that works, copy-ready examples for the most common automations, and the checks to run before you turn anything on.

The short answer

Open Flow in your Shopify admin, click the Sidekick icon, and describe the automation in one sentence that names a trigger, a condition, and an action. For example: "When a customer places an order over $200, tag them as VIP." Sidekick drafts the workflow and leaves it inactive. Review every step, test it, then click Turn on workflow. Nothing runs until you do.

What the Shopify Flow AI assistant is

Flow is Shopify's native automation tool. Every workflow follows one shape: a trigger fires, a condition filters whether it should run, and an action does the work. The AI assistant is Sidekick working inside Flow. It became generally available in December 2025 and was extended in early 2026 alongside Flow's redesign and safe-preview testing.

Sidekick's job is to pick the right trigger, condition, and action from a library of hundreds, so you do not need to know their names first. You describe the outcome and it drafts the workflow into the editor for review.

It is not a general AI agent for your store. It builds Flow workflows and nothing else. It cannot edit your theme, change a product page, or write landing page copy. And it does not run anything by itself: every workflow it drafts starts inactive, tagged "sidekick", and waits for you to turn it on.

How to create a workflow with the AI assistant

Step 1: install Flow if you have not already. Flow is free on the Basic, Grow, Advanced, and Plus plans. Open Apps in your Shopify admin, then Flow.

Step 2: click the Sidekick icon in the workflow editor and describe the automation in plain language. Shopify's own example reads: "I want to create a workflow that tags orders with fewer than 3 line items as 'small'." One or two sentences is enough.

Step 3: Sidekick builds the workflow and summarizes how it works, with a button to open it. Click through to the canvas instead of trusting the summary. This walkthrough of the Flow AI steps follows the same five-step sequence Shopify documents.

Step 4: review every node. Check that the trigger matches the event you meant, the condition operator matches your intent, and every tag or email address is spelled exactly as it exists in your store.

Step 5: test, then activate. Run the workflow against a test event or sample data first, then click Turn on workflow. Two limits to know before you start: creating workflows with Sidekick works on desktop only, and the Send HTTP Request action is limited to Grow plans and above, while custom partner app tasks need Plus.

The prompt formula: trigger, condition, action

Every prompt that works follows the same shape: trigger (what starts the workflow) plus condition (the rule that filters when it runs) plus action (what should happen). Add the exception last: what should stop the workflow from running.

Weak: "Tag my VIP customers." Sidekick has to guess the trigger, the threshold, and the tag, and a guess is usually wrong in at least one place.

Strong: "When a customer places an order over $200, tag them as VIP." The trigger is an order placed, the condition is over $200, the action is the VIP tag. Nothing is left to guess.

Three rules make the formula work. First, give the operator and the value: "greater than $500" beats "high value". Second, use Flow's action verb: "add the order tag" beats "flag it". Third, state every wait: Flow infers no timing, so if the email should go out 7 days after delivery, say 7 days.

Copy-ready prompt examples

Copy any of these into the Sidekick panel inside Flow and change the numbers, tags, and email addresses to match your store. Each one follows the trigger, condition, action shape.

Orders and fraud: "When Shopify's risk analysis rates an order as high risk, tag the order 'review' and email the team." Check the tag spelling before you activate, and remember this runs after fraud analysis finishes, not the moment the order is placed.

VIP customers: "When a customer places their third order, tag them as 'loyal' and send a thank-you email with a 10 percent discount code." The discount code must already exist in your store before the workflow runs.

Inventory alerts: "When a product variant's available quantity falls below 20, email ops with the product title, SKU, and quantity." This fires on every stock movement, so expect one email per unit sold until you add a condition to limit it.

Back in stock: "When a product variant is back in stock, send the customer who signed up for the restock alert an email." Check which app owns the restock signup before you rely on this trigger.

Scheduled report: "Every Monday at 8 a.m., email me a list of variants with fewer than 10 units that sold more than 30 units in the last 30 days." Scheduled prompts need the day, the time, and both numbers stated plainly.

Small orders: "When an order is created with fewer than 3 line items, tag the order as 'small'." This is Shopify's own example prompt, and it is a good first test because the result is easy to check.

Prompts that fail and how to fix them

Vague nouns fail first. "Flag big orders" has no trigger, no number, and no action verb. Rewrite it as: "When an order is created over $500, add the tag 'big-order'." If a prompt has no number and no exact value, Sidekick invents one, and invented values are wrong more often than they are right.

Missing exceptions fail second. "Email me when stock is low" fires for every variant in your catalog. Add the exception: "Do not run for products tagged 'discontinued'." One exception line can cut a hundred unwanted emails a day.

Multi-branch logic fails third. Sidekick is strongest at tagging, segments, notifications, inventory alerts, and scheduled reports. It handles deep multi-branch logic poorly and struggles with actions that need Liquid or custom code. For those workflows, ask Sidekick for the simple version, then build the branches by hand on the canvas.

Wrong names fail last. If your tag is "VIP-Customer" and the prompt says "VIP", the workflow creates a second tag instead of matching the first. Check every tag, segment name, and email address against what actually exists in your store before you activate.

What to check before you turn a workflow on

Read the cards, not the summary. The assistant's summary is often right while a condition operator is wrong, so open each node and check the operator and the value. "Greater than" and "greater than or equal to" are different rules, and the difference matters at exactly $200.

Run a test event. Sidekick can generate one, or you can pick an existing order or product. Watch the full execution path in preview mode and confirm the action fires only when it should.

Check the blast radius. A workflow that emails a customer or changes a price affects real shoppers the first time it runs. Start with a narrow condition, watch the first few runs, then widen it.

Note the plan limits. The Send HTTP Request action needs a Grow plan or above, and custom partner app tasks need Plus. If Sidekick drafts a workflow your plan cannot run, the action will fail, so check the plan column before you activate. Frequently asked questions

Is Shopify Flow free?

Yes. Flow is free on the Basic, Grow, Advanced, and Plus plans. Two actions carry plan limits: Send HTTP Request needs Grow or above, and custom partner app tasks need Plus.

Does Sidekick turn workflows on by itself?

No. Every workflow Sidekick builds starts inactive and tagged "sidekick". Nothing runs on your live store until you review it and click Turn on workflow.

Can I use the Flow AI assistant on my phone?

No. Creating workflows with Sidekick works on desktop only. The feature does not appear in the Shopify mobile app.

What happens if my prompt is vague?

Sidekick guesses the missing pieces: the trigger, the threshold, or the tag. A guessed workflow usually gets at least one detail wrong, so name the trigger, the condition with a number, and the action in every prompt.

Can Sidekick edit my theme or products?

No. Sidekick inside Flow builds workflows only. Theme edits, product changes, and landing pages are outside what it can do.
