Enterprise Dropship Software vs Shopify Dropshipping Apps

Enterprise dropship software and Shopify dropshipping apps solve the same problem at different scales. One is an operations layer for retailers with many suppliers and channels. The other is a connector that puts products into a single Shopify store. This guide compares cost, supplier connections, order routing, and multichannel support, so you can pick the side that fits your volume.

A Shopify store owner who wants to sell without holding stock faces two very different tools. On one side are the Shopify dropshipping apps: DSers, Spocket, AutoDS, Zendrop, and CJ Dropshipping. On the other are enterprise dropship platforms: Flxpoint, Spark Shipping, Rithum, and Logicbroker. Both promise to automate the same work, but they are built for different businesses.

The difference is scale, and it shows up in the price, the supplier connections, and the order routing. Pick the wrong side and you either pay enterprise prices for a ten-product store, or you run a thousand-SKU catalog on an app that was never built for it. This guide compares the two side by side, with real prices checked in November 2026, so you can see which one fits your volume.

The short answer

If you run one Shopify store with a handful of suppliers and a small catalog, use a Shopify dropshipping app. DSers is free to start, Spocket starts at $39.99 a month, and AutoDS starts at $26.90 a month. That is the cheapest way to test dropshipping, and it is enough for most new stores.

If you run hundreds or thousands of SKUs across five or more suppliers, sell on more than one channel, or work with distributors who send inventory by EDI or FTP feed, you need enterprise software. Spark Shipping starts at $299 a month for 200 orders, and Flxpoint Business is $799 a month for 3,000 orders. The price is higher because the job is bigger: one central catalog, automated routing, and stock that stays in sync everywhere you sell.

The short version: apps are for starting, enterprise software is for operating at volume. The middle ground, a Shopify storefront with an enterprise backend, is where many growing stores end up, and it is covered in section 9.

What "enterprise dropship software" actually means

Enterprise dropship software is the operations layer behind a store, not a store itself. It takes supplier catalogs, cleans up the product data, keeps stock and prices in sync, routes each order to the right supplier, and pushes tracking back to the customer. It does not sell anything on its own. Your storefront, Shopify or otherwise, is where customers buy.

The names in this space are Flxpoint, Spark Shipping, Rithum, Logicbroker, and SPS Commerce. Flxpoint is built for midsize retailers and distributors with many dropship vendors. Spark Shipping serves small to midsize stores that automate dropshipping across several vendors. Rithum, Logicbroker, and SPS Commerce serve large retailers running big EDI vendor programs, and they price on request rather than publishing a plan.

What these platforms share is the supplier connection. They connect to the distributors you already work with, through EDI, API, FTP, CSV, or email feeds, and they handle catalogs of tens or hundreds of thousands of SKUs. That is a different job from importing a product from a marketplace, and it is why the pricing starts in the hundreds per month instead of free.

What Shopify dropshipping apps actually do

A Shopify dropshipping app lives inside your Shopify admin and does three things: it imports products into your store, it keeps their prices and stock in sync, and it places the order with the supplier when a customer buys. Most of them also give you a catalog to pick products from, so you do not need a supplier relationship of your own to start.

DSers is the closest replacement for Oberlo, which Shopify retired in 2022. It imports from AliExpress and 1688 and places orders in bulk. Spocket is a marketplace of vetted suppliers, many in the US and Europe, with delivery typically in 2 to 7 days. AutoDS connects to more than 25 supplier sources and watches prices and stock for you. Zendrop leans on US warehousing and custom packaging. CJ Dropshipping offers factory pricing and private warehousing, with slower shipping from China.

The key point is where the products come from. With an app, you mostly pick products from the app's own catalog or from AliExpress. With enterprise software, you bring your own suppliers and their feeds. That single difference decides most of the comparison below.

Cost comparison: what each side really charges

Start with the apps, because the numbers are small. DSers Basic is free for 3 stores and 3,000 products, Advanced is $19.90 a month, and Pro is $49.90 a month. Spocket runs from $39.99 to $99.99 a month. AutoDS starts at $26.90 a month, with auto-ordering as a $9.90 a month add-on. Zendrop charges $29, $49, or $79 a month depending on how many products you link. CJ Dropshipping is free to join and you pay per order, with membership tiers from $15.99 a month. Syncee has a free plan and paid plans from $39.99 a month.

Enterprise software starts higher and charges for volume. Spark Shipping starts at $299 a month for 200 orders and three supplier connections, with 39 cents for each order after that. Its Growth plan is $499 a month for 1,500 orders. Flxpoint Business is $799 a month, or $699 a month billed annually, for 3,000 orders, and Pro is priced on a call. Inventory Source sits in between: a free supplier directory, paid plans from $99 to $399 a month, and Flxpoint as its enterprise upgrade. Rithum, Logicbroker, and SPS Commerce do not publish prices at all.

Both sides sit on top of a storefront cost. A Shopify store starts at $39 a month for the Basic plan, and that cost applies whether you use an app or an enterprise platform. Enterprise setups can also carry onboarding and custom connection fees, so ask for the full quote, including setup, before you sign. The cheapest honest comparison is total monthly cost at your order volume, not the headline price of one tool.

Supplier connections: EDI and feeds vs marketplaces

Enterprise platforms connect to suppliers the way distributors actually send data: EDI, API, FTP, SFTP, CSV, and XML feeds. A distributor sends a feed with its full catalog, prices, and stock levels, and the platform updates your store from it, often several times a day. When a supplier is not prebuilt, enterprise platforms still take the feed, with setup fees for custom API or EDI connections.

Shopify apps connect to suppliers a different way: through their own marketplace or through AliExpress. You search the catalog, import the products you want, and the app keeps those listings in sync. That is fast to start, and it is the right model when you have no supplier relationships yet. It is the wrong model when you already have distributors, because their catalogs do not live in any app marketplace.

The question to ask is simple: do your suppliers already exist, or do you need to find them? If they exist and send feeds, you need software that reads feeds. If you need to find them, an app marketplace is the faster start. This comparison of multi-supplier inventory platforms shows how the enterprise tools handle supplier connections side by side.

Order routing and inventory at scale

Order routing is where the two sides separate. Enterprise software routes each order by rule: cheapest supplier with stock, closest warehouse, or a priority order you set. It can split one customer order across two suppliers, hold backorders, and push tracking numbers back to the store and the customer automatically. At hundreds of orders a month, that routing is the whole job.

Most Shopify apps route simply: one product, one supplier, one order. Auto-ordering exists, but it is an add-on, and multi-supplier routing rules are not what these apps are built for. That is fine at 20 orders a day from a small catalog. It breaks when the same product comes from three suppliers at three prices and only one of them has stock today.

Inventory sync is the same story. Enterprise platforms sync stock across every channel you sell on, so a sale on Amazon updates the same stock your Shopify store shows. Apps sync stock for the products they imported, in your Shopify store, and that is usually enough until you sell in more than one place.

Multichannel selling: where each side breaks

Enterprise software is built for selling in several places at once. One central catalog feeds Shopify, Amazon, eBay, and Walmart, with channel-specific prices and listings. That is the core promise of platforms like Flxpoint and Rithum: list once, sell everywhere, and keep stock honest across all of it.

Shopify apps are mostly built for Shopify alone. AutoDS is the exception, with support for eBay, Amazon, Walmart, and TikTok Shop alongside Shopify. DSers, Spocket, and Zendrop are Shopify-first. If your plan is one Shopify store, that is not a limitation. If your plan is Shopify plus Amazon plus eBay, an app will leave you managing three separate catalogs by hand.

So the multichannel question is a fork in the road. One store, one app. Several channels, enterprise software, or a hybrid where the enterprise platform sits behind the Shopify storefront.

Who should pick which side

Pick a Shopify dropshipping app if you are starting out, you have one Shopify store, your catalog is under a few hundred products, and your suppliers come from AliExpress or a US and EU marketplace. Your monthly software cost stays under $100, you can be selling this week, and you can switch later without losing the store.

Pick enterprise dropship software if you already have supplier relationships, your catalog runs into the thousands of SKUs, you work with five or more suppliers, you sell on more than one channel, or your suppliers send inventory by EDI or FTP feed. At that point the $299 to $799 a month buys back more time than it costs, because manual order routing and stock checks no longer scale.

The common mistake is buying enterprise software too early. A store doing 30 orders a month does not need order routing rules, EDI feeds, or a central catalog. It needs products, traffic, and a first sale. Start with an app, and move to enterprise software when the volume forces the move, not before.

The hybrid setup: Shopify storefront plus enterprise backend

The most common setup for a growing dropshipping store is a hybrid: Shopify stays the storefront, and an enterprise platform runs the operations behind it. Flxpoint and Spark Shipping both connect to Shopify, so the storefront keeps Shopify checkout, themes, and apps, while the backend handles supplier feeds, routing, and multichannel listings.

This setup makes sense once the store is doing real volume but the owner does not want to rebuild the storefront. The cost is the Shopify plan plus the enterprise platform, so it is the most expensive option on paper, but it is also the one that scales without a rebuild. If you are planning this move, do it before the catalog grows past what you can migrate by hand, not after.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use enterprise dropship software with Shopify?

Yes. Flxpoint and Spark Shipping both connect to Shopify, so the storefront stays on Shopify while the enterprise platform handles supplier feeds, inventory sync, and order routing behind it. That hybrid setup is the most common way growing stores adopt enterprise software.

Is DSers free?

Yes, the Basic plan is free and covers 3 stores and 3,000 products, but it does not sync price and stock automatically. Advanced is $19.90 a month and Pro is $49.90 a month, and automatic sync starts on the paid plans.

How much does enterprise dropship software cost?

Spark Shipping starts at $299 a month for 200 orders. Flxpoint Business is $799 a month, or $699 a month billed annually, for 3,000 orders. Rithum, Logicbroker, and SPS Commerce price on request. Inventory Source sits in between, with paid plans from $99 to $399 a month.

Do Shopify dropshipping apps hold inventory?

No. Neither the apps nor the enterprise platforms hold stock. The supplier holds the stock and ships the order. The software only moves the product data, the order, and the tracking information between the supplier and your store.

Which is better for a beginner?

A Shopify dropshipping app. DSers is free to start, Spocket starts at $39.99 a month, and you can list products the same day. Enterprise software only pays off once you have the order volume and supplier count that make manual work slow.

Can I switch from an app to enterprise software later?

Yes, and most growing stores do. Export the catalog from the app, connect the suppliers to the enterprise platform, and move the listings over. Do the move before the catalog is too large to migrate by hand, and keep the Shopify storefront so the store URLs do not change.
