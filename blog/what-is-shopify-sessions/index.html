What Is a Shopify Session? A Simple Guide for Store Owners

A Shopify session is one visit to your store by one shopper. Here is what it means, how Shopify counts it, and how to use it to grow sales.

If you run a Shopify store, you have probably seen the word "sessions" on your analytics dashboard. It is one of the first numbers Shopify shows you, right next to conversion rate and total sales.

But many store owners are not sure what a session actually counts. Is it the same as a visitor? A page view? And why should you care? This guide explains it in plain terms, and shows how to use your session numbers to make better decisions for your store.

What a Shopify session actually is

A session is one visit to your store by one shopper. When someone opens your store in their browser, Shopify starts a session. When they leave, close the tab, or sit idle for 30 minutes, the session ends. If the same person comes back later that day, that counts as a second session.

Think of your store like a physical shop. A session is one person walking through the door. They might come in three times in one week, and each visit counts separately. That is exactly how Shopify counts it.

Sessions vs visitors vs page views

A session is not the same as a page view or a visitor. Page views count every page load, so one shopper who looks at ten pages creates ten page views but only one session. A visitor is the person, while a session is one of their visits.

The conversion rate you see on your dashboard is orders divided by sessions. This is why sessions matter more than raw visitor counts. They tell you how many buying opportunities your store actually got. If 100 sessions turned into 2 orders, your conversion rate is 2 percent.

How Shopify counts a session

Shopify starts a session the moment someone lands on any page of your store. It keeps counting as long as the person keeps browsing. After 30 minutes of no activity, Shopify closes the session. A new session also starts if the same person returns from a different source, like clicking a link from an email after finding you on Google earlier.

This is useful to know because it means your session count reflects real browsing behavior. A session that includes several product pages and a cart view is a much warmer prospect than a session that bounces after three seconds.

Why sessions matter for your business

Session data becomes powerful when you combine it with other numbers. Look at sessions by source: are your Instagram sessions converting worse than your Google search sessions? That tells you where your best customers come from.

Also watch sessions over time. A steady rise in sessions with flat sales means you are getting traffic but losing people on the site. A drop in sessions usually means a marketing channel dried up or a campaign ended. Both patterns point you to a concrete fix instead of guesswork.

What to do when sessions look wrong

If sessions are rising but orders are not, start with your product pages. Slow load times, unclear pricing, or missing shipping information are the usual suspects. Check the product pages that get the most session traffic first, since small fixes there have the biggest impact.

If sessions are falling, check your marketing one channel at a time. Which source dropped the most? Often it is as simple as a paused ad, an ended promotion, or a social post that stopped getting reach. Restart what was working before you try something new.

Frequently asked questions

How long is a Shopify session?

Shopify counts one session per browser tab, and it ends when the shopper is inactive for 30 minutes, closes the tab, or leaves your site. A return visit starts a new session.

Does one session mean one product viewed?

Roughly. Shopify counts any session where the visitor viewed at least one product page, but the exact product viewed is not shown. Bots and internal traffic filters can shift the numbers slightly.

Why do my sessions differ between Shopify and Google Analytics?

Sometimes. A large gap usually means your marketing reports or analytics tags count visits differently, for example a different timeout length or different bot filtering.
