Can You Sell Peptides on Shopify?

You can open a Shopify store and list peptide products, but you cannot run it safely for long. Shopify Payments blocks the category, the Acceptable Use Policy lets Shopify close the store, and no label or third-party gateway changes either rule. Here is what the rules actually say, and the compliant way to sell peptides online.

Peptides are one of the most searched health products online, and Shopify is the first platform most sellers think of. The two do not mix. You can open a Shopify store and list peptide products, but you cannot run that store safely for long, because the rules that decide the question sit in Shopify Payments and in Shopify's Acceptable Use Policy, not in the store builder.

This guide gives the straight answer, then the detail: what the rules actually say, which peptides are covered and which are not, what happens to stores that get flagged, and the compliant way to sell peptides online instead.

The short answer

No. You cannot sell research peptides on Shopify in any safe, lasting way. Shopify Payments prohibits pseudo-pharmaceuticals and products that require regulatory approval, which is the category research peptides fall into. Shopify's Acceptable Use Policy then gives Shopify room to close the store itself, even if you use a different payment processor. A store can look perfect and still be a policy violation from day one.

That answer covers research peptides: BPC-157, TB-500, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, and GLP-1 analogues sold as research chemicals. It does not cover every product with the word peptide in it. Cosmetic peptides, such as copper peptides in serums and collagen peptides in skincare, are mainstream products and generally fine on Shopify. The rest of this guide explains where the line sits.

What Shopify's rules actually say

Start with a correction to something you will read everywhere: Shopify's Acceptable Use Policy does not use the word peptides. We read the policy, and it is written as principles, not a product list. It says you cannot use Shopify for anything illegal where you do business, you must follow the agreements you signed including payment terms, and you must act in good faith rather than gaming the rules to avoid constraints.

The specific ban lives one layer down, in payments. Shopify Payments runs on Stripe, and Stripe's prohibited businesses list names two categories that cover research peptides directly: "incorrectly labeled research chemicals" and "pseudo-pharmaceuticals or nutraceuticals that are not safe or make harmful claims." Shopify's own payments eligibility rules add products that require regulatory approval and prescription drugs. Put together, a research peptide store is blocked at the payment layer and exposed at the platform layer at the same time. Read the sources yourself: Shopify's Acceptable Use Policy and Stripe's prohibited and restricted businesses list.

That two-layer structure is the whole story. The platform rule means Shopify can remove the store. The payment rule means no mainstream processor will settle the money. Neither rule cares how good the store looks.

Research peptides vs cosmetic peptides: the distinction that matters

The word peptide covers very different products, and the rules treat them very differently. Getting the distinction right is the difference between a store that runs for years and one that gets closed in weeks.

Research peptides are compounds sold for laboratory or research use, usually as vials or capsules, with names like BPC-157, TB-500, CJC-1295, and ipamorelin. They are the high-risk case. Regulators treat many of them as unapproved drugs the moment a seller implies human use, and payment providers classify them as research chemicals or pseudo-pharmaceuticals.

Cosmetic peptides are ingredients in skincare: copper peptides in serums, signal peptides in eye creams, and collagen peptides in creams and supplements. These are mainstream products sold by large brands, and they do not trigger the same rules, as long as the claims stay cosmetic and modest.

Peptide supplements sit in between. A substance that is approved as a drug, or authorized for investigation as a new drug with substantial clinical investigations under way, generally cannot be sold as a dietary supplement unless it was marketed as a supplement or food first. On August 24, 2026, the FDA warned a seller that a peptide supplement was adulterated because it contained a new dietary ingredient. If your product sits in this middle ground, get legal advice before you list it anywhere.

Why the FDA angle matters

Shopify's rules point back to the regulator, so the FDA position is what makes the payment rules stick. The FDA treats many marketed peptides as unapproved new drugs once a seller implies they are for human use. BPC-157 and TB-500 are Category 2 substances, which means they are prohibited from compounding because of safety concerns.

This is why the Acceptable Use Policy matters even if you never touch Shopify Payments. The policy says you cannot use Shopify for anything illegal where you do business. A product the FDA classes as an unapproved drug is, by that definition, a problem for the platform too, and Shopify does not need a payment dispute to act on it.

What happens when a store gets flagged

Enforcement rarely starts with a warning email asking you to fix a listing. The sequence merchants describe is consistent enough to plan around, even though the details vary by account. Payouts pause first. The store may keep taking orders for a short window while settlement has already stopped, which is the worst possible state to be in, because you are taking on obligations you cannot fund. Then the store or the payments account is disabled, and the balance goes into a hold.

Fund holds in this situation are commonly reported in the 90 to 180 day range, and in some cases the money is never released. Account termination can be permanent, and a banned merchant cannot simply open a new store and start again.

What usually triggers the review is ordinary business activity, not a complaint: a volume increase that pushes the account into manual review, a chargeback pattern, a card-network inquiry, or a routine risk sweep that reads the product catalog. Shopify also scans listings automatically for prohibited content, so a store does not need a customer complaint to be found.

Why "research use only" labels do not protect you

The most common workaround sellers try is labeling: "research use only," "not for human consumption," or a disclaimer on the product page. It does not work. The category decision is made by the payment underwriter and the platform, not by the label on the bottle. Neither Shopify Payments nor Stripe changes its answer because of a disclaimer.

The heavier workarounds are worse. Renaming SKUs to something vague, miscoding products, listing an unrelated item as the checkout product, or splitting the real catalog onto a decoy storefront are all violations of the same agreement you are already under. Shopify's policy expects merchants to act in good faith, and trying to game the rules is itself grounds for account-level action. A store caught doing this loses the store and the argument.

Why a third-party payment gateway does not save the store

The next idea sellers try is skipping Shopify Payments and plugging in a third-party gateway. It does not fix the problem, for two reasons. First, Shopify's Acceptable Use Policy applies whatever gateway you connect, so the store can still be removed on the platform rule alone. Second, most high-risk gateways are not approved Shopify payment apps in the first place, so the setup is fragile even before anyone reviews it.

It also costs more on every order. When you do not use Shopify Payments, Shopify adds a third-party transaction fee: 2% on Basic, 1% on Grow, 0.6% on Advanced, and 0.2% on Plus, as of November 2026. So a peptide store on Shopify would pay extra per order for a payment setup that still leaves the store exposed. That is the worst of both worlds.

The compliant path: WooCommerce and a high-risk merchant account

The route built for this category is a self-hosted store plus a high-risk merchant account. WooCommerce is self-hosted, so the store software imposes no category policy of its own. The payment side is a high-risk merchant account from an acquirer that knowingly underwrites research-use-only peptides, connected to checkout through a gateway such as Authorize.net or NMI.

Expect underwriting to take time and the terms to be stricter than a normal store: the site must pass review, and merchants report rolling reserves of 5 to 10% of monthly volume and transaction fees two to three times higher than low-risk categories. That is the real cost of selling in this category, and it is better to pay it above board than to lose a store and months of payouts to a freeze.

The migration itself is a known job: map the URLs, move the catalog, then switch the payments, the same steps our WooCommerce to Shopify migration guide walks through in the other direction. If you are starting from scratch, build on WooCommerce first and skip the Shopify detour entirely.

What you can sell on Shopify instead

If you like the health and wellness niche but want a store that lasts, Shopify is still the right platform, in a compliant niche. Fitness supplements such as collagen and creatine, nootropics, mushroom coffee, and skincare with cosmetic peptides are all established categories with real demand. The rule is the same one that keeps any health store safe: keep the claims modest, stay inside what the product is actually recognized for, and do not promise medical outcomes.

Supplements still draw scrutiny, especially subscriptions and auto-ship programs, so read the payments rules for your exact products before you build. A short policy check before you invest in a store is far cheaper than a freeze after launch.

Frequently asked questions

Can you sell peptides on Shopify?

No, not research peptides. Shopify Payments prohibits pseudo-pharmaceuticals and products that require regulatory approval, and Shopify's Acceptable Use Policy lets Shopify close the store even if you use another payment processor. Cosmetic peptides in skincare are a different case and generally fine.

Does Shopify's policy actually name peptides?

No. The Acceptable Use Policy does not use the word peptides. It is written as principles, and the specific categories come from the payment rules: Stripe's list names incorrectly labeled research chemicals and pseudo-pharmaceuticals, and Shopify Payments bans products that require regulatory approval. Research peptides fall under those categories.

Can you sell cosmetic peptides on Shopify?

Yes, generally. Copper peptides in serums, signal peptides in eye creams, and collagen peptides in skincare are mainstream cosmetic ingredients. Keep the claims cosmetic and modest, and do not promise medical results.

Does a "research use only" label make it allowed?

No. The label does not change the category decision made by the payment underwriter or the platform. Disclaimers do not protect a store, and disguising the products is a separate violation that makes the outcome worse.

Can a third-party payment gateway fix it?

No. Shopify's Acceptable Use Policy applies whatever gateway you connect, so the store can still be removed. On top of that, Shopify charges a third-party transaction fee of 2% on Basic, 1% on Grow, 0.6% on Advanced, and 0.2% on Plus, so you pay extra for a setup that still leaves the store exposed.

Where should peptide sellers build instead?

WooCommerce with a high-risk merchant account. WooCommerce is self-hosted and imposes no category policy of its own, and a high-risk acquirer that knowingly underwrites research-use-only peptides can process payments through a gateway such as Authorize.net or NMI. Expect stricter terms, including a rolling reserve.
