How Long Does It Take to Build a Shopify Website?

A basic Shopify store can go live in 1 to 3 days when your products and brand details are ready. A standard theme build takes 1 to 3 weeks, and a custom build takes longer. Here is where the time goes and how to launch faster.

Shopify is one of the fastest ways to get a store online, and that speed is half the reason people choose it. But "fast" covers a wide range: one store launches in a weekend, another takes two months, and both owners did nothing wrong.

The honest answer is that a basic Shopify store can go live in 1 to 3 days when your products, images, and brand details are ready. A standard store built on a bought theme usually takes 1 to 3 weeks. A custom build by a freelancer runs 2 to 6 weeks, and a custom agency build runs 2 to 4 months. Shopify itself puts a website build at anywhere from one week to five months. This guide breaks down where the time goes, so you can tell which timeline fits your store.

The short answer

If you need one number, use this: most small stores launch in 1 to 3 weeks. A store with a handful of products and a ready theme can be live in a few days. A store with hundreds of products, custom design, and integrations needs weeks, and an agency-level custom build needs months.

Three things decide where you land in that range: how many products you sell, how much custom design you want beyond the theme, and how ready your content is. The platform itself is rarely the slow part. Stores that take months are almost always waiting on product data, a theme decision, or content, not on Shopify.

Timeline by store type

Basic DIY store, 1 to 3 days. You pick a free theme, add your products, set up payments and shipping, and launch. This works when your product photos and descriptions are ready and you are happy with the theme mostly as it is.

Theme-based store, 1 to 3 weeks. You buy a paid theme, customize the layout, colors, and fonts to match your brand, and load a real catalog. Paid themes in the Shopify Theme Store run from $210 to $450, and most popular ones sit between $280 and $380. This is the timeline most new stores should plan around.

Custom build by a freelancer, 2 to 6 weeks. Custom design, custom Liquid code, and features the theme does not offer take real development time. Add a week or two for finding and onboarding the freelancer before work starts.

Custom build by an agency, 2 to 4 months. Agencies handle design, development, and integrations as a team, which is right for a large catalog or a Shopify Plus store, but the process has more stages and more people to brief.

For context, a 2026 survey of more than 300 web development companies cited by Shopify puts a typical ecommerce website at 8 to 28 weeks. A Shopify theme build sits well below that range because the platform handles hosting, checkout, and the store framework for you.

What each phase actually takes

Account setup and settings, 4 to 8 hours. Create the account, pick a plan, and set up the basics: store name, currency, taxes, and policies. Shopify offers a 3-day free trial on its paid plans, so you can start the build before you pay.

Theme selection and customization, 1 to 3 days for basic changes. Choosing a theme takes an hour or two if you decide quickly. Customizing it, logo, colors, fonts, homepage sections, and navigation, takes about 4 to 8 hours. Heavy custom code stretches this to 5 to 7 days or more.

Products and content, 1 to 5 days. This is where most builds slow down. Adding a product takes about 5 to 10 minutes when the photos and description are ready, and 30 minutes or more when you are writing from scratch. Twenty products is an afternoon. Two hundred is a week.

Payments, shipping, and taxes, 2 to 4 hours. Connect a payment provider, set shipping rates and zones, and check tax settings. Most stores finish this in half a day.

Domain, up to 2 days. Pointing your own domain at Shopify usually takes effect within a couple of hours, but Shopify documents that DNS changes can take up to two days. Start this on day one so it does not hold up launch.

Testing and launch, 1 day. Place test orders, check the store on your phone, and confirm emails and checkout work. Then remove the password page and launch.

The three things that slow a build down

Product data is the biggest delay. Every product needs a photo, a price, a description, and usually a variant setup. Stores that launch late are usually still writing product copy or waiting on photos, not waiting on a developer.

The theme decision is second. Browsing themes is fun, and it is easy to spend a week comparing them. Pick a theme that already looks close to what you want, because the fewer structural changes you need, the faster you launch. If you want help matching a theme to your brand, our theme customization service covers layout, sections, and design fixes.

Approvals and content are third. A build stalls when the owner has to approve every design draft or write every page from scratch. If you hire a developer, answer feedback fast and have your logo, brand colors, and product photos ready before work starts.

DIY or hire a developer: how the timeline changes

Doing it yourself is fastest to start and slowest to finish if the job is big. You start the same day, there is no waiting for a developer, and a small store can be live in under a week. The cost is your own time, and the risk is a store that looks unfinished.

Hiring a freelancer takes 2 to 6 weeks from start to finish, including communication, design drafts, and revisions. You also spend 1 to 4 weeks finding and onboarding the right person before work begins. The build itself is faster in skilled hands, but the total calendar time is longer.

An agency takes 2 to 4 months for a custom build. That is the right timeline for a store that needs several skills at once: design, development, SEO, and integrations. For a small store, an agency is usually more process than the job needs.

How to launch faster without cutting corners

Launch with the smallest version that can take an order, then improve it. Shopify's own setup checklist has 53 tasks, but only four decide whether you can sell: a paid plan, a working payment method, shipping rates, and store policies. Everything else can be polished after launch.

Prepare your content before you start the build. Write product descriptions, gather photos, and decide on your logo and colors first. A build with ready content moves at twice the speed of one waiting on it.

Use a theme that fits out of the box. Every structural change costs time, so start close to your target design instead of rebuilding a theme from scratch.

Skip apps you do not need yet. Each app adds setup time and a possible conflict to debug. Add apps after launch, one at a time, when you know what problem each one solves.

Set a launch date and work backwards. Without a date, a build expands to fill the time available. A date forces decisions, and decisions are what keep a build moving.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to build a Shopify website from scratch?

A basic store with ready products can go live in 1 to 3 days. A standard theme-based store takes 1 to 3 weeks, a custom freelancer build 2 to 6 weeks, and a custom agency build 2 to 4 months.

Can I build a Shopify website in one day?

Yes, if your products, photos, and descriptions are ready and you are happy with a free theme mostly as it is. You still need a paid plan, a payment method, shipping rates, and store policies before you can take a real order.

What takes the most time when building a Shopify store?

Product content. Adding a product takes 5 to 10 minutes with photos and descriptions ready, and 30 minutes or more when you write from scratch. A large catalog is what turns a one-week build into a month-long one.

Does hiring a developer make a Shopify build faster?

It makes the skilled work faster, but the total calendar time is longer: 2 to 6 weeks for a freelancer and 2 to 4 months for an agency, plus time to find and onboard them. DIY starts immediately but depends on your own hours.

How much does it cost to build a Shopify website?
