Hit by a Google Update? Thin Pages, Doorway Pages and How to Recover

My own site was hit for having too many location pages. Here’s what I learned and the recovery plan I now use with stores.

By Saqib AliPublished 2026-09-129 min read

Contents

  2. What Google is penalizing
  3. Finding the problem pages
  4. The recovery plan

What happened to my own site

The previous Globe Skillz site had a page for nearly every city and industry combination: “SEO for dentists in Ohio”, “web design in Florida”, and many more. Each one was a slight variation of the others. They ranked for a while. Then an update arrived and the whole site dropped.

That’s a textbook doorway page pattern, and Google is right to devalue it. The site you’re reading now is the fix: fewer pages, each describing a service I actually offer, backed by real work.

What Google is penalizing

  • Doorway pages: many near-identical pages targeting keyword or location variations, all leading to the same thing
  • Scaled content: pages produced in bulk (by AI or templates) mainly to rank, with little original value
  • Thin pages: e-commerce examples include empty tag pages, filter URLs and collections with one or two products
  • Unhelpful content: pages written for search engines rather than people, without first-hand experience

Finding the problem pages

  1. Export every indexed URL from Search Console and your crawler
  2. Group them by template, such as products, collections, tags, blog and pages
  3. For each group, check clicks over the last 12 months. Large groups with almost no clicks are the main suspects.
  4. Read a sample. If several pages could swap their keywords and still read the same, you’ve found the pattern.

The recovery plan

  • Remove pages with no value and no links (return 410, or noindex if they’re needed for navigation)
  • Merge near-duplicates into one strong page and 301 redirect the rest
  • Improve the pages that remain with expertise, original images, specifics and an author
  • Wait for the next core update. Recovery usually shows up then, not right away.
Honest timeline: recovery often takes until the next core update, which means months. Removing bad pages quickly and improving the remaining ones is what gets a site ready for that update.
