Follow the money

One Install Pays for a Year of Marketing

Here is the math most plumbing companies never do. An emergency callout is fast money: burst pipe, call, fix, invoice. But it is also the most competitive search in the trade, the lowest ticket, and a customer you may never see again. A boiler install, a repipe, a bathroom rough-in: slower to win, worth many times the callout fee, and it often turns into a customer for life. Yet almost every plumber I audit markets exclusively for emergencies and leaves the big jobs to whoever wrote the most helpful guide.

Those big jobs are won months before the emergency ever happens, through content. The homeowner researching tankless water heater pros and cons, or what a boiler installation costs, is not calling anyone today. They are learning. And when they are ready, they call the plumber whose page taught them something, not the one with the loudest ads. Your competitors are almost certainly not writing this content. That gap is the opportunity sitting open in your market.

So the strategy has two lanes, and I build both. Lane one: instant visibility for emergencies, the local pack, the reviews, the tap-to-call button, because when water is where it should not be, the searcher calls whoever looks legitimate in five seconds. Lane two: authoritative pages for the planned work, the installs and renovations that actually move your revenue. Most plumbers fight over lane one. Lane two is often empty, and it is where the money is.

Week one

The 14-Point Audit I Run Before Writing a Single Word

Before I write anything, I take your entire online presence apart. Here is the actual checklist, so you know exactly what the word audit means when I say it:

  • Google Business Profile categories: are you listed as a plumber, or buried under a generic contractor category that Google barely understands.
  • Service area definition: the actual towns you serve, set properly, not a hundred-mile radius fantasy that dilutes everything.
  • Hours and emergency availability: stated clearly, including after-hours, so nobody has to guess whether you will pick up.
  • Review velocity and recency: not just the total count, but whether new reviews keep arriving every month.
  • Tap-to-call on mobile: your number reachable in one tap from every page, above the fold, no hunting.
  • Mobile page speed: emergency searchers are on phones, often on slow connections, always in a hurry.
  • One page per money job: emergency, installs, drains, renovations, each with its own dedicated page.
  • Service-area pages: real local detail per town, never find-and-replace templates.
  • Title tags and headings: written for humans who are searching, not stuffed with keywords.
  • Schema markup: LocalBusiness and Plumber structured data, implemented correctly.
  • Internal linking: service pages linked the way a customer actually navigates, not the way a sitemap looks tidy.
  • Photo proof: real job photos, real vans, real faces. Stock imagery convinces nobody.
  • Competitor gaps: what the top three in your market are missing, which is usually plenty.
  • Call tracking: so we know which pages and searches produce real jobs, not just rankings.

You get every finding in plain language, ranked by impact. Most plumbing companies are shocked at how much of this is broken, and how fixable it all is. The audit alone usually surfaces wins that cost nothing to implement.

Keyword architecture

Emergency Searches and Planned Searches Need Different Pages

One plumbing services page cannot rank for everything, because the searches have nothing in common. Emergency plumber near me is typed with water on the floor. Boiler installation cost is typed from a sofa on a Sunday afternoon. I build separate pages for separate intents, and each one is designed around what that searcher needs in that exact moment.

The emergency page is built for speed: what counts as an emergency, your response times, your service area, and tap-to-call everywhere. It exists to convert a panicked visitor in under a minute. The installation pages are built for education: honest pricing guidance, process explanations, and FAQ content answering what homeowners ask before they call. This content pre-qualifies callers, so you spend your quoting time on serious jobs instead of tire-kickers.

Drain and sewer work gets its own pages too: drain cleaning, sewer line repair, camera inspections. Unsexy, high-margin, and searched with very specific intent that generic pages miss entirely. Same for bathroom and kitchen work: renovation plumbing, fixture installs, remodel support. Every page earns its place by matching a real search with real intent. The same trust-first approach works across the trades; the home services companies I work with sell the same thing, a stranger in your home, and where water damage is involved the restoration playbook overlaps with ours.

Speed wins

The 60-Second Emergency Test

Pick up your phone, search emergency plumber near me, and open your own site. Start a timer. Can a panicked stranger find your number, understand that you handle emergencies, and call you within sixty seconds, on a cracked screen, with one thumb, standing in water. Most plumbing sites fail this test. The number is in the footer. The homepage talks about commitment to excellence. The emergency page does not exist.

Fixing it is unglamorous and decisive. Number and call button at the top of every page, on mobile, always visible. A dedicated emergency page that says what you do, where, and how fast. After-hours availability stated plainly, with an answering setup that actually answers. Whether you cover one town or run crews across the USA, the test is the same, and most of your competitors are failing it right now. Passing it is worth more than any ranking trick I know.

The review engine

Reviews Are Your Second Website

Two plumbers side by side in the local pack. One has a steady stream of recent, specific reviews mentioning real jobs. The other has thirty reviews, the newest from last year. The choice is made before either website loads. Reviews do two jobs at once: they lift your map rankings and they close the sale for the next panicked searcher.

The system is simple: ask every happy customer at the right moment, the day the job is done, with a direct link that takes ten seconds. I build the system and write the templates; your team sends the ask, because it lands best coming from the person who did the job. Then respond to every review, good and bad, like a professional. Steady and specific beats big and stale, every time.

And a word on spending. Some plumbers pay monthly for SEO on terms they already dominate, or for blog content nobody in their service area will ever read. I audit what you have first and cut what is not earning its keep. A website with no phone number above the fold, review silence after great jobs, chasing only emergency terms while ignoring the planned work: these are the leaks I fix before spending a cent on anything new.

The long game

What Month Six Looks Like

Plumbing SEO compounds in a way most owners do not expect. Month one is foundations: the audit fixes, the profile rebuild, the review system going live. Months two and three are the service pages going up, each one targeting a job you actually want. Around month four or five, something shifts: the review count starts doing its own selling, the planned-work pages start pulling in researchers who turn into install quotes, and the emergency visibility steadies.

I will not tell you which month the phone starts ringing, because that depends on your market, your competition, and how fast we implement. Anyone who names a month is guessing. What I will tell you is what we track from day one: calls, not just rankings. Which pages produce enquiries, which searches produce jobs, and where the next effort goes. You will see the compounding happen in your own numbers, and that is a better promise than any timeline I could invent.

The pricing question

Should You Put Prices on Your Plumbing Website

Every plumber asks me this, and the honest answer is: it depends on the job. For standard, repeatable work, published pricing wins. A call-out fee, stated plainly. Fixed prices for the common jobs. The homeowner comparing three plumbers at 9pm will shortlist the one who answered the money question and skip the ones who hid it. Transparency is a conversion tool, not just a nice trait.

For complex work, installs, repipes, renovations, exact prices are impossible and publishing them would be dishonest. What works instead is honest ranges plus a clear explanation of what moves the number: boiler type, property size, access, the state of the existing pipework. A sentence like most boiler installs we do fall in this range, with real numbers from your own past jobs, beats both silence and false precision. It pre-qualifies callers, so the people who phone are already comfortable with the ballpark.

From an SEO perspective, pricing content is pure gold. Searches like how much a boiler installation costs are typed constantly, by people close to buying, and most plumbers refuse to answer them. A straightforward pricing guide, updated yearly, will outrank and out-convert almost anything else on your site. Your competitors' silence is your opening. Take it.

A final note on the phone itself. All of this content, the pricing guides, the service pages, the reviews, exists to produce one action: a call. If your site makes calling even slightly difficult, perhaps a contact form with too many fields standing between the searcher and your number, or a mobile layout where the call button disappears on scroll, fix that before you publish another word. I check this on every audit and it is broken more often than you would believe. The fanciest content strategy in the world cannot compensate for a phone number that is hard to find at the moment it matters most.
