SEO EXPERT · ROMANIA

Why Romania businesses choose me

If you run a business in Romania, your customers are searching for what you sell right now, in Romanian, on Google. That sentence sounds obvious, but most Romanian businesses still act like it is not true. They have a website that was translated from English, or built five years ago and never touched, while a competitor down the street collects the calls. Romania is the largest EU country in Southeastern Europe by population, with around 19 million people, and its internet economy has been accelerating for a decade. Bucharest concentrates the money, the multinationals and the agencies. Cluj-Napoca has grown into the country's second tech capital, full of software companies and startups. Timisoara anchors the west with automotive software and engineering, and Iasi does the same in the northeast with IT and outsourcing. Together these four cities hold most of the country's commercial search demand, and in three of them, proper SEO is still rare enough that a well-run campaign can win fast. The Romanian buyer is a specific animal and you have to respect how they shop. eMAG, the dominant ecommerce platform, trained millions of Romanians to compare prices, read reviews and check ratings before spending a single leu. That comparison habit does not stop at products. When someone in Bucharest needs a lawyer, a dentist or a renovation crew, they open Google, compare the top results, read the reviews, and only then call. They are price-aware but not cheap: they will pay for quality once they understand what they are paying for. Vague pages that hide pricing lose to clear pages that explain value. This is why generic SEO advice imported from the US or UK underperforms here. It was written for buyers who behave differently. Language is the other half of the story. Romanian dominates local search, and Romanian has diacritics that change meaning: s and t with a comma below appear in everyday words. Search engines handle them, but most business websites do not. They publish in English, or they machine-translate, and they wonder why they never rank for the queries real customers type. I plan every campaign around genuine Romanian keyword research, and the pages read naturally to native speakers because they are written for them from the start, not converted afterward. The competitive picture is the good news. Google handles the overwhelming majority of Romanian searches, so there is one battlefield, not five. Outside Bucharest, most local competitors have thin websites, incomplete Google Business Profiles and almost no reviews. That is a gap you can drive through. I work as a one-person studio, which means you get the person doing the work, not an account manager relaying messages. The audit is free, the plan is written in plain language, and the first wins usually show up where the competition is thinnest: your own city, in your own language.

I work with only a few clients at a time, so each Romanian business gets hands-on work and direct answers — no ticket queues, no junior account managers.
