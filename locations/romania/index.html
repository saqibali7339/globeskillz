Why Romania Needs a Different SEO Playbook

If you run a business in Romania, your customers are searching for what you sell right now, in Romanian, on Google. That sentence sounds obvious, but most Romanian businesses still act like it is not true. They have a website that was translated from English, or built five years ago and never touched, while a competitor down the street collects the calls. Romania is the largest EU country in Southeastern Europe by population, with around 19 million people, and its internet economy has been accelerating for a decade. Bucharest concentrates the money, the multinationals and the agencies. Cluj-Napoca has grown into the country's second tech capital, full of software companies and startups. Timisoara anchors the west with automotive software and engineering, and Iasi does the same in the northeast with IT and outsourcing. Together these four cities hold most of the country's commercial search demand, and in three of them, proper SEO is still rare enough that a well-run campaign can win fast.

Romania looks like a standard European market from a distance, and that is exactly why most SEO campaigns fail here. They are built on assumptions that do not hold. The first wrong assumption is language. Around 19 million people live in Romania, and the commercial searches that matter are typed in Romanian, with Romanian diacritics, using Romanian phrasing. 'Cel mai bun' beats 'best', 'preturi' beats 'pricing', and a page written in English and run through a translator will never feel native to the buyer or rank like a native page. I have seen businesses spend thousands on English content aimed at Romanian customers and get nothing, because the customers were never searching in English in the first place.
Facts on the ground

Local intelligence for Romania.

  • Google handles the overwhelming majority of searches in Romania, with industry estimates putting its share near 97 percent, so winning on Google effectively means winning the Romanian internet.
  • Romania is the largest EU country in Southeastern Europe by population at around 19 million people, and it completed full Schengen integration at its land borders in March 2025.
  • The Romanian IT sector employs more than 150,000 people and contributes an estimated 6 to 7 percent of GDP. Global names like Bitdefender and UiPath were engineered by Romanian talent.
  • Bucharest is the business capital, while Cluj-Napoca, Timisoara and Iasi form a triangle of fast-growing tech hubs with rising demand and thinner SEO competition than the capital.
  • Romania uses the leu (RON), not the euro, so prices, schema markup and ad copy for the local market must be written in lei to feel native.
  • eMAG dominates Romanian ecommerce and has trained shoppers to compare prices, read reviews and check ratings before they buy anything, a habit that carries over to local services.
  • Romanian buyers search in Romanian with diacritics (s, t with comma below), and sites that handle Romanian spelling properly have a quiet technical edge over translated English pages.

01 / Playbook

Citations and Directories: the Romanian Foundation

Citations are the boring foundation that rankings are built on, and in Romania most businesses have a cracked one. Your business is listed in dozens of places across the web: Google Business Profile, Pagini Aurii (the Romanian Yellow Pages), Yelp, Facebook, sector-specific directories, old portals nobody remembers signing up for. Over the years those listings drift. An old address here, a tracking phone number there, a duplicate profile from when the business changed names. Google cross-checks your name, address and phone number everywhere it can find them, and when the listings disagree, Google trusts you less. In a market where few competitors have clean citations, cleaning yours is a genuine competitive advantage. My citation work for Romanian businesses follows a fixed order. First, the Google Business Profile gets claimed, verified and fully completed: correct primary category in Romanian search terms, every relevant secondary category, services listed, real photos, accurate hours including holiday schedules. Second, I audit the full citation footprint and fix inconsistencies, starting with the directories that actually matter in Romania: Pagini Aurii, Yelp Romania, Facebook, and the sector directories for your trade. I skip the junk directories that exist only to sell links, because they do nothing for rankings and can create more mess than they solve. Every listing ends up with the same +40 phone number, the same address format, and the same business name spelling, diacritics included. Then there is the review layer, which in Romania sits on top of citations like a multiplier. Pagini Aurii and Google both show ratings, and Romanian buyers are trained by ecommerce to filter by them. I set up a simple review routine for your business: the right ask at the right moment after a job is done, a direct link that makes leaving a review take thirty seconds, and genuine replies to every review that arrives. Over months this compounds into the kind of public proof that makes choosing you feel obvious. Citations get you into the consideration set; reviews win the comparison. I build both, because in Romania you need both.

02 / Playbook

Winning the Map Pack in Romania

For most local searches in Romania, the buying decision happens before anyone reaches a website. The map pack shows three businesses, and the customer picks from those three. That is the whole game for local SEO here, and it is why I treat your Google Business Profile as your most important web property, not an afterthought. Romanian searchers use queries like 'instalator Bucuresti', 'stomatolog Cluj preturi' and 'restaurant langa mine', and the businesses that win those queries share the same traits: complete profiles, correct categories, recent reviews, real photos, and posts that show the business is alive. Optimizing a profile for the Romanian market has specific details that generic guides miss. Categories need to match how Romanians search, not how Google translates your trade into English. Service areas need to reflect real travel patterns: someone in Cluj-Napoca will drive to Floresti but not to Turda for most services, and your profile should say so. Opening hours need to be accurate down to the Saturday schedule, because 'deschis acum' (open now) filtering is heavily used and wrong hours lose you the click at the exact moment of intent. Photos need to show the real business, the real team, the real work, because Romanian buyers have learned to distrust stock photography the same way they distrust vague pricing. Reviews are the tiebreaker in the Romanian map pack, and the dynamics are specific. A profile with 4.5 stars and fifty genuine reviews will outrank an older competitor with twelve reviews, even if that competitor has been around longer. Recency matters: a review from last month counts more than one from two years ago. I build the review engine into your daily operation so it runs without willpower: QR codes on invoices, a direct link sent by SMS after the job, replies to everything within a day. And because Romania is a market where word travels fast in professional circles, I make sure your responses to negative reviews are calm, specific and solution-oriented. Handled well, a critical review answered properly converts better than a page of five-star praise.
