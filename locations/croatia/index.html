Local SEO · Croatia

Croatia has two economies, and your SEO has to serve both.

As an SEO expert in Croatia, the first thing I tell business owners is this: you are not running one business, you are running two. From June to September, the Adriatic coast belongs to visitors. Germans, Brits, Poles and Italians flood Dubrovnik, Split, Hvar and Rovinj, phones in hand, searching for restaurants, boat tours, apartments and experiences in their own languages. Then October arrives, the crowds leave, and a completely different Croatia takes over: Zagreb's year-round services economy, where locals search in Croatian for dentists, lawyers, plumbers and gyms.

Most Croatian business websites I audit are built for only one of these worlds. The coastal hotel has a beautiful site in English but nothing that ranks for a Zagreb local searching on a Tuesday in February. The Zagreb service company ranks decently at home but is invisible to the tourist standing 200 meters from its door in July. That split is the whole game here, and it is why generic SEO advice, written for one-language, one-season markets, keeps failing in Croatia.

I build search strategies around both Croatias. Seasonal pages that capture the summer surge in multiple languages, and evergreen local pages that keep the phone ringing through winter. When both halves work, you stop dreading October.

The coast

What summer visitors search for, and what they never find

A tourist in Split does not search like a local. They search "best seafood restaurant Split old town" or "boat trip Hvar tomorrow" in English, German or Italian, usually on mobile, usually with booking intent inside the hour. If your site only exists in Croatian, or buries its booking page three clicks deep, you simply do not exist for that search. I have watched businesses with prime locations lose entire seasons to competitors with worse food and better English landing pages.

The fix is not translating your whole site. It is building a small set of sharp, multilingual landing pages aimed at the exact searches visitors type, each with instant booking or WhatsApp contact. A wedding venue near Dubrovnik, for example, can win foreign couples who plan destination weddings a year ahead, but only with English content that answers their real questions about permits, weather and vendors.

The capital

The Zagreb market most outsiders ignore

While the coast sleeps in winter, Zagreb works. Nearly a quarter of the country's population lives in the capital region, and they search the way people search everywhere: "zubar Zagreb" (dentist), "automehanicar" (car mechanic), "najbolji restoran" (best restaurant). Croatian-language search dominates here, which is actually good news, because most of your competitors have thin, outdated sites.

Zagreb is also where Croatia's B2B economy lives. If you sell to other businesses, from Slovenia to the coast, your buyers are searching from offices in Zagreb, in Croatian, during working hours. A Google Business Profile tuned for the right categories, plus service pages written in natural Croatian rather than translated corporate English, will put you ahead of most of the local competition within months.

Language

The quiet ranking factor: Croatian first, English second.

Croatian is a small language on the internet, and that is an opportunity. There is far less content competing for Croatian queries than for English ones, which means a well-built page in proper Croatian can rank with a fraction of the effort an English page needs. But "proper" matters. I regularly see sites running machine-translated Croatian that reads like a puzzle. Google's language models have gotten good enough to tell, and Croatian customers definitely can.

My rule for Croatia: your money pages exist in Croatian first, then you add English (and where it pays, German and Italian) versions for the tourist-facing parts of the business. Hreflang tags keep the versions from competing with each other. Done right, you rank for "smjestaj Dubrovnik" and "accommodation Dubrovnik" without either page cannibalizing the other.

  • Croatian-language search dominates, with tourism queries spiking along the Adriatic coast.
  • Dubrovnik, Split and island resorts create extreme summer seasonality in demand.
  • Zagreb holds the year-round business and services market inland.
  • Visitors search in many languages, so multilingual pages win bookings.
