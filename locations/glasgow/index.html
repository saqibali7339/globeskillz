Should my Glasgow business target neighbourhood keywords like “West End” or just “Glasgow”?

Both, but structured properly. Your homepage and main service pages target Glasgow-level terms, while dedicated neighbourhood pages capture searches like “plumber Shawlands” or “restaurant Finnieston” — which convert better because the searcher has already narrowed their location. Businesses that only target “Glasgow” leave the highest-intent searches to competitors.

How do matchdays at Celtic Park and Ibrox — and gigs at the Hydro — affect local search?

They create predictable, repeated surges in hospitality, hotel, parking, and takeaway searches around the venues and transport corridors. Smart Glasgow businesses plan for it: Google Business Profile posts ahead of big fixtures, event-relevant content, and review pushes timed to busy weekends. It’s scheduled demand most competitors ignore.

Is it harder to rank in Glasgow than in Edinburgh?

In most consumer niches, yes. Glasgow is Scotland’s largest city with a denser field of competitors, and its hospitality and trades markets are among the UK’s most contested. Edinburgh has its own pressures — tourism seasonality and London firms competing for capital-city keywords — but pound for pound, Glasgow’s map pack is the tougher fight.
