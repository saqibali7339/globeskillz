Local SEO · USA

The USA is fifty search markets wearing one flag.

As an SEO expert in USA markets, the most expensive mistake I see is the national template: one website, one strategy, applied identically from Miami to Seattle. It fails because America does not search identically. A plumber in Houston competes in a sprawling metro where "near me" means a 20-minute drive; a plumber in Portland, Maine serves a compact city where three competitors own the map. Spanish-language search is a major channel in Texas, Florida and California and nearly irrelevant in Vermont. Review culture, directory habits, even the words people type, shift by region.

The businesses winning American local search understand this. They run location-aware strategies: pages and profiles tuned per market, review generation that never stops, and content written in the language their customers actually use, including Spanish where the data supports it. The ones losing are running a single generic site and wondering why the phone favors their competitors.

I plan US campaigns market by market. Whether you own one shop or operate across states, the principle is the same: be the most relevant, most reviewed, most complete result for the searches that happen where your customers are.

Two models

Single-city shop vs multi-state brand: the strategy splits here.

Model A

The single-location business

Your whole game is the map pack. One perfect Google Business Profile, relentless review generation, and a website whose location pages prove you are the local authority. Speed matters more than size here: a garage door company in one metro can own its market with a dozen excellent pages and a hundred genuine reviews, because most local competitors have neither. Budgets stay focused, results arrive fast, and every ranking directly rings your phone.

Model B

The multi-state operator

Scale changes everything. You need consistent listings across every location, location pages that are genuinely different from each other (not find-and-replace clones, which Google discounts), a review system that works at every branch, and centralized technical health. The prize is bigger, dozens of map packs instead of one, but so is the operational discipline required. I build the systems and the page frameworks; your team executes locally.

Most US businesses I audit sit uncomfortably between these models: big enough to need systems, small enough to lack them. The audit sorts out which model fits, and we build accordingly. Either way, the fundamentals do not change, they just scale.

The map pack

In America, the map pack is the whole game.

Google handles the overwhelming majority of US searches, and for local intent the map pack sits above everything. Three businesses, picked by proximity, relevance and prominence. American customers are comparison shoppers: they read reviews like research papers, checking recency, detail and how you respond to complaints. Review quantity, recency and detail directly shape who gets shortlisted, which is why review generation is not a nice-to-have here, it is the campaign.

"Near me" searches are the other American signature. A huge share of service discovery happens this way, on mobile, with immediate intent. Winning "near me" means accurate service areas, correct categories, and location pages that confirm relevance. I have seen businesses double their call volume without ranking a single new keyword, purely by fixing their map presence. If you serve Spanish-speaking customers, Spanish-language pages and profile posts open a demand channel most competitors leave untouched, as I also see in markets like Spain.

  • "Near me" searches are a core part of how Americans find services, from plumbers to restaurants.
  • Spanish-language search represents significant demand in Florida, Texas, California and across the Southwest.
