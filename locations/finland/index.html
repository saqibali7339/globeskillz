Field notes

Five mistakes I keep finding on Finnish websites.

I have audited enough Finnish business sites to see the patterns repeat. As an SEO expert in Finland, these are the five issues I find most often, roughly in order of how much business they cost.

Mistake 01

English pages targeting Finnish searches

The site is in English. The customers search in Finnish. Google Finland shows Finnish results for Finnish queries, so the English site never appears. This alone explains a shocking number of invisible Finnish businesses.

Mistake 02

One page trying to cover every service

A single "services" page listing plumbing, heating, and renovations ranks for none of them. Finns search for the specific problem: "putkimies helsinki", not "our services". Each service needs its own page, in Finnish, answering real questions.

Mistake 03

A Business Profile nobody finished

Claimed years ago, wrong categories, three blurry photos, no posts, unanswered reviews. In Finland's map-driven local search, a half-built profile is a closed sign. Competitors with complete profiles take the calls.

Mistake 04

No reviews, or reviews never answered

Finns read reviews carefully and distrust businesses with none. Worse is a profile with a one-star review sitting unanswered for a year. A calm, professional reply in Finnish turns a complaint into proof you care.

Mistake 05

Ignoring the Swedish-speaking coast

Finland is officially bilingual, and the coastal areas search in Swedish. Businesses there that add Swedish pages pick up customers their competitors never see. It is a small effort for a real edge in Vaasa, Turku, and the capital region.

Language

Finnish is a different beast entirely.

Finnish is unrelated to Swedish, English, or German. Words change form with fifteen cases, compounds run long, and search terms rarely look like their dictionary entries. Keyword research for Finland cannot be translated; it has to be built from how Finns actually type. "Lvi asentaja" and "ilmalämpöpumppu" are not guesses, they are the product of looking at real Finnish search data.

This is why template SEO fails here. An agency translating English keywords into Finnish produces phrases nobody searches. I build Finnish keyword maps from Finnish search behaviour, then write pages in natural Finnish that match how customers describe their own problems. It is slower work than translating, and it is the entire reason it works.

Language shapes every market I work in. My approach as an SEO expert in Sweden deals with Swedish compounds; in Finland the grammar itself is the puzzle.

Regional markets

Helsinki, Tampere, and the Lapland winter spike.

Helsinki region

Where the competition lives

Half of Finland's commercial search happens in the capital region. Espoo and Vantaa included, this is the competitive core: agencies, chains, and funded startups. Winning here needs depth, but the search volume justifies the effort for most B2B and high-value services.

Tampere and Turku

Strong demand, thinner field

Finland's second cities have real local search volume and far fewer businesses doing SEO well. For trades, clinics, and services, Tampere and Turku are often the fastest route to page one in the country.

Lapland

Seasonal gold

Winter tourism drives an enormous seasonal spike: safaris, glass igloos, northern lights tours. The businesses that prepare content and profiles before autumn own the winter. Seasonal SEO planned six months ahead beats panic spending in November every time.

The east and north

Quiet but winnable

Smaller cities, smaller volumes, almost no SEO competition. A single well-built Finnish page can dominate a whole region for years. For local trades, this is the closest thing to free visibility left in Europe.

Content

The content Finns actually read.

Finns research before they buy, more thoroughly than almost any market I work in. A Finn choosing a heat pump installer will read three comparison articles, check forum discussions, and look at review profiles before ever picking up the phone. Thin marketing copy does not survive that process. Detailed, honest content does.

So the content I build for Finnish businesses is built to withstand scrutiny: real answers about costs, timelines, and trade-offs, written in natural Finnish. A page that honestly explains when your service is not the right choice will outsell a page that claims you are perfect for everyone, because Finnish buyers can tell the difference and they reward the honesty with their business.

This is also why Finnish content keeps working for years. A genuinely useful Finnish guide to choosing a service earns links from forums, gets bookmarked, and keeps ranking long after the trends change. Quick translated blog posts decay; honest depth compounds.
