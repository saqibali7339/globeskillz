Yes — and each one needs to earn its place. A thin 'Exeter' page and a thin 'Torquay' page will both underperform; what works is a substantial page per location with genuine local proof (local photos, local testimonials, location-specific services and access notes), linked from a county hub page. That structure builds topical authority for 'Devon' terms while each page fights its local battle. One generic page trying to cover both cities ranks for neither.
You plan for it in autumn. Winter content — off-season breaks, locals' offers, Christmas and New Year packages in Torquay, winter wedding deals — needs to be live by October to rank for December searches. And keep your review engine running through the quiet months: winter guests' reviews keep your profile looking alive to February bookers planning summer trips. The businesses that go dark in November spend March catching up.
One profile with honest service areas can cover a service-area business, yes — but a profile for every town you don't genuinely operate from will get you suspended. The honest playbook: one profile per real location, service areas set truthfully, and win with reviews, photos and town-specific website content rather than fake pins. For single-location businesses, own your patch completely before expanding — depth beats breadth.
It's some of the best-value SEO in the county. Village-specific searches like 'farm shop South Hams' have low volume but extremely high intent, and almost no rural businesses do SEO properly — so a rebuilt Google profile, a few genuine local pages and a steady review flow can dominate your patch within months. Low competition plus high-intent buyers is exactly where a small budget goes furthest.
Specific towns first, 'Devon' second. Town terms ('wedding venue Barnstaple', 'plumber Exmouth') have lower competition, clearer intent and faster wins — and they're what your customers actually type. Build town pages with real local proof, link them from a county hub page, and the 'Devon' authority follows naturally. Targeting 'Devon' generically from day one is the most common mistake I see: it reads as being about nothing in particular, and Google treats it that way.
