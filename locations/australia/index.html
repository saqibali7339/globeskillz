Start here

The pre-flight checklist I run on every Australian site.

An SEO expert in Australia has to deal with a particular kind of customer: review-driven, allergic to marketing fluff, and quick to punish businesses that overpromise. I like that. It means the work that wins is honest work. Before I change a single headline, I run every Australian site through the same checklist, because most ranking problems I find are unglamorous basics done badly.

  • Profile completeness. Google Business Profile with the right primary category, every service listed, real photos of real work, and opening hours that are actually correct. Most Aussie profiles I audit fail at least two of these.
  • Review quality, not just stars. Australian customers read reviews properly. Ten detailed recent reviews beat fifty one-liners. I set up a simple post-job routine so good reviews keep arriving.
  • Australian English throughout. Content written in American phrasing quietly undermines trust and misses real search terms. I write the way your customers type.
  • Service areas that make sense. Accurate service areas matter more here than in most countries because of the distances involved. Claiming suburbs you will not drive to wastes everyone's time.
  • Mobile speed. A huge share of local searches happen on phones, often on patchy connections outside the metros. If your site stumbles on mobile, the map pack will not save you.
  • One page per real offering. Not one page per suburb. Dedicated pages for each service you actually sell, written to answer the questions customers ask before they call.
Common failures

Where Australian businesses usually leak rankings.

Leak 01

Set-and-forget profiles

The profile was claimed in 2019 and never touched again. Wrong hours, three blurry photos, services never listed. Meanwhile a competitor posts weekly updates and answers every question. Google notices the difference, and so do customers. Profiles are living assets; neglected ones quietly sink.

Leak 02

Review silence

No review strategy at all, or worse, a burst of reviews two years ago and nothing since. Recency matters. I build review collection into the natural end of your job process so it happens without awkwardness, and I make sure every review gets a genuine reply.

Leak 03

Generic national content

Blog posts written for nobody in particular, targeting nobody's actual search. Australian customers distrust fluff, and Google increasingly agrees. I replace filler with pages that answer the specific questions your local customers type, in their own phrasing.

Leak 04

Ignoring the regions

Trades and home services are fiercely competitive in Sydney and Melbourne but wide open in regional areas. Businesses that build proper regional pages, with honest service areas and real local proof, often find their easiest wins an hour outside the capital.

Trade businesses feel these leaks hardest, and plugging them is what an SEO expert in Australia should have done on day one. If you run one, see how I approach pest control companies and lawn care companies. And if you operate across the Tasman or further afield, my UK pages follow the same no-fluff principles.

Geography

Sydney is not Perth: distance changes everything.

Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth and Adelaide function as separate local search markets, and the gaps between them are enormous by European standards. That has practical consequences most agencies gloss over. Service-area settings need to reflect real drive times, not wishful thinking. Content that mentions "servicing all of Australia" reads as noise; content that speaks to a specific city reads as relevant.

It also changes the competitive picture. What works against Sydney competition (relentless reviews, deep service pages, constant profile activity) is overkill for a regional centre where three complete profiles would own the map pack. As an SEO expert in Australia, I calibrate effort to the market: heavy artillery where you need it, efficient basics where you do not. That is the advantage of working with a one-person specialist instead of an agency selling one package at one price.

The through-line is honesty. Accurate areas, real photos, genuine reviews, content in Australian English that answers real questions. Do that consistently and the map pack, which decides most local service enquiries here, starts working for you instead of against you.
