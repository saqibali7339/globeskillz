France questions, answered.

Straight answers about French SEO, no sales talk. If your question is not here, the free audit covers it.

Yes, and it affects your SEO data directly. The CNIL is Europe's most active cookie regulator: in September 2025 it fined Google 325 million euros and Shein 150 million euros for consent violations. Your banner must make refusing as easy as accepting, consent is valid for about 13 months, and scripts must actually be blocked until the visitor agrees. Most French sites I audit fail at least one of these. I fix the banner and the tag firing order together, so your analytics become both compliant and accurate.
Yes, but within strict limits. Lawyers (avocats), doctors and other regulated professions answer to their ordres, which restrict comparative claims, superlatives and sometimes even client testimonials. That does not rule out SEO: informational content, local relevance, reviews on permitted platforms and a strong Google Business Profile are all allowed. I build strategies that stay inside the rules, because a sanction from your ordre costs far more than any ranking is worth.
As a citation, yes. PagesJaunes is still a trusted source of business data that Google reads when verifying your existence, and a correct listing there supports your local rankings. As an advertising channel, it depends on your sector. I always claim and correct the listing, because it is free trust signal work, and then I judge paid placement on its own merits. What you should never do is rely on PagesJaunes instead of your Google Business Profile, which is where the customers actually decide.
Start where your customers are. If you serve one city, dominate it first: a plumber in Lyon should own Lyon before thinking about Paris. Paris is the biggest prize and the most competitive market in the country, with national brands and big budgets fighting over every keyword. Regional cities like Bordeaux, Lille, Toulouse and Nantes often deliver faster wins with genuinely local content and reviews. My audits map the competition per city so I attack where the return is highest first.
Very carefully. The avis section of your Google Business Profile is often the deciding factor: French customers read reviews before calling, and they notice whether you reply. A steady flow of recent French-language reviews, with thoughtful owner responses, will beat a competitor with a nicer website and an empty profile. I set up a review process that asks in French at the right moment, because in France the business with the best-reviewed profile usually gets the call, not the business with the best-designed site.
