THE HONEST PICTURE

Agency or freelancer: an honest look at both

Where agencies are usually stronger

  • More people, so more capacity and more disciplines under one roof.
  • Continuity if a staff member leaves.
  • Established processes and formal contracts, which some companies require.

Where a specialist is usually stronger

  • The person who sells you the work is the person who does it.
  • Faster decisions and changes, often the same day.
  • More of your budget goes to SEO work rather than overhead.
  • Flexible scope: an audit, a one-off project, or a monthly retainer.

The honest part: where a specialist is weaker

  • Limited capacity. One person can only serve a small number of clients well.
  • Single point of failure: if I'm unavailable, work slows down.
  • Narrower scope: I don't run paid ads, branding or video.

Neither model wins in every case. What matters is which one matches your situation.

USE IT ON EVERYONE

The 7-point checklist for choosing any SEO provider

Use these questions with RegalSEO, with me, and with anyone else on your list. They're the same questions I'd want you to ask me — a good provider welcomes all seven.

And watch for the red flags: guaranteed rankings or “page one in 30 days,” refusing to explain what they do, pressure to sign a long contract before showing any work, vague answers about who does the work, or reports with no explanation of what was actually done.

1

Who will actually do the work?

Ask for names and roles, not “our team.” If the person you meet on the sales call won't be doing the work, find out who will.

2

Can I see a real sample report?

A good provider can show a redacted report that explains what changed, why, and what happens next. Be wary of reports that are only screenshots of ranking tools.

3

Can I speak to a current or past client?

References should be real and reachable. Case studies with numbers and timeframes are a good second-best.

4

What exactly am I paying for each month?

Ask for a written scope: audits, content pieces, links, hours. Vague packages are hard to hold anyone to.

5

What happens if I cancel?

Look for the notice period, any minimum term, and whether you keep the work, access and documentation.

6

How do you build links?

Ask for the method and examples. Anyone who says “guaranteed links” or is vague about sources is a risk. Poor link building can hurt your site.

7

How will we measure success?

Agree on the goals up front: leads, revenue and qualified traffic, not just rankings. Ask how long results usually take. Honest answers are measured in months, not weeks.

MAKE IT OBJECTIVE

A simple way to score your options

Score each provider from 1 to 5 on the seven questions above and total them. Weight the points that matter most to you — a small business may care most about who does the work and cancellation terms, while an enterprise may care most about capacity and references.

1. Who does the work
2. Sample report quality
3. Reachable references
4. Written monthly scope
5. Fair cancellation terms
6. Honest link building
7. Clear success metrics

If a provider scores poorly on more than two, keep looking — whichever model they use.
