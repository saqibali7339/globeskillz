The collection page is your most important SEO asset

Someone searching “linen bedding sets” or “gold hoop earrings” wants to see options, not a single product. That’s why Google usually ranks collection pages for these searches, and it’s why I spend most on-page time on collections.

Anatomy of a collection page that ranks

  1. H1 that matches the search: “Gold Hoop Earrings”, not “Shop All Hoops”
  2. About 60 words above the grid explaining what’s in the range
  3. The product grid, unchanged so shoppers still get to products quickly
  4. A buyer’s guide below the grid covering sizes, materials and how to choose
  5. An FAQ answering real questions from your support inbox
  6. Links to related collections to help both shoppers and crawlers

Proof: CTR is a ranking lever

For a lashes and brows brand, rewritten titles, descriptions and rich results brought click-through rate to 5.2%, well above the store’s average, while positions stayed about the same. More clicks from the same rankings is the quickest win in SEO.

FAQ

Collection & Product SEO questions

Have a different question? Ask me on WhatsApp.

How much text should a Shopify collection page have?

Enough to answer the shopper’s questions. That’s usually 50–80 words above the grid and 300–700 words of guide and FAQ below it. Length on its own doesn’t rank, but helpful content does.

Do you use AI to write product descriptions?

I use AI for research and first drafts, then write and edit every page by hand for accuracy, your brand voice and real product knowledge. Unedited AI copy is the kind of thin content Google’s updates target.

Will adding content to collections slow them down?

No. Text adds almost nothing to page weight. I also place below-the-grid content so it never pushes products down on mobile.

Can you work in languages other than English?

Keyword research and structure, yes, for any market. Copy in other languages is written together with native-speaking writers.
